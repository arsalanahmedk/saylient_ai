Politeness theory, in brief

A short overview of the research behind Saylient's rewrites — just the ideas that shape every suggestion.

The idea in one paragraph

Politeness theory (Brown & Levinson, 1987) says every request risks the reader's "face" — their wish to be liked, and to not be imposed on. Saylient keeps your ask intact and softens how it lands, choosing the warmth, hedging or directness that fits the tone you pick. Later work (Watts, 2003) treats politeness as ongoing relational work — which is why Saylient offers tones to choose from, not one fixed rewrite.

Face

Positive face wants approval; negative face wants freedom from pressure. A good rewrite protects both.

Five strategies

From bald-on-record directness to a gentle off-record hint — the theory's five ways to deliver an ask.

Four tones

Professional hedges politely, Friendly adds warmth, Firm stays direct but face-saving, Short trims to the act itself.

Overview based on Brown & Levinson (1987), Watts (2003), and Locher & Watts (2005).
